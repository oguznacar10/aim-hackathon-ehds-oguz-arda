# Competitor landscape: who builds Health Data Access Body software, and where we can stand out

Status: PROPOSED. Everything in this file is a proposal for Arda and Oguz to accept or reject. Nothing here is decided.
Written: 2026-10-30, 01:00 to 01:45 CET, by a research session. All web pages were read on 2026-10-30 unless a line says otherwise.
Method: three read-only web workers (public bodies, vendors and open source, prior art and papers) summarised pages; the orchestrating session read the TEHDAS2 D6.4 PDF itself with pdftotext and judged the rest. Workers reported no page that addressed an AI or asked for an action. Facts a worker saw only in a search snippet, not on the page, are marked "via search summary". Facts nobody saw in a primary source are marked "unverified".

Capability key used in every table:
- C1 = request intake and a REJECT / APPROVE_STATISTICAL / APPROVE_SPE decision with EHDS article citations
- C2 = anonymised statistics with the small-cell rule (counts below 10 shown as "<10", small percentages and means "suppressed", every requested group shown)
- C3 = secure processing environment (SPE) holding pseudonymised records with downloads blocked
- C4 = separate applicant and staff views

## Bottom line

- Nobody ships all four capabilities as software today. The EU's own reference specification for the access-body application system (TEHDAS2 D6.4 "DAAMS") puts the two things we build, the decision logic and the SPE, explicitly out of scope.
- The biggest real-world competitor is Findata (Finland): a live, closed service that does all four in practice, by hand, with human output checkers. It is the thing an access body would compare us with.
- The nearest look-alike code is a single-maintainer GitHub demo (ma3u/MinimumViableHealthDataspacev2, Apache-2.0) with an "HDAB Authority" approval workflow and an SPE. It cites articles loosely (Art. 45-53) and has no small-cell rule and no Art. 73 download block.
- The clearest gap: a decision that quotes the article paragraph that applies (54(b), 66(3), 68(3) second subparagraph) and shows the 68(3) downgrade from pseudonymised data to anonymised statistics as an explicit, explained outcome. No product, spec or paper found does that.
- PROPOSED USP: "the explained decision". Deterministic rules pick the decision and the article list; the reason text quotes the paragraph; the statistics table and the blocked download are shown in the same flow as consequences of that decision. The AI angle is a language model that drafts the reason and flags borderline cases, never one that chooses the articles.

## 2. What none of them do that a jury notices in a 2-minute demo

1. The decision quotes the paragraph. DAAMS records a human's justification as free text and a signed PDF (R7.3.1, R7.3.7, R7.3.8); DUOS cites ontology codes; the Al Mandalawi paper cites enterprise controls; ma3u cites "Art. 45-53" as a block. Nobody shows "REJECT, Art. 54(b): insurance premiums" or "APPROVE_SPE, Art. 66(3): applicant demonstrated anonymised data cannot achieve the purpose" generated from the request itself.
2. The 68(3) downgrade as a visible outcome. An access application that fails Art. 66(3) does not just get refused; the body may answer with anonymised statistics under Art. 69 if the applicant agrees. No tool found shows this route. Two legal jurors will recognise it; the brief's R09 is exactly this case.
3. One flow, three consequences. Today the decision (Findata staff, REMS, HDR UK), the suppressed table (ACRO, OpenSAFELY, OHDSI) and the download block (Aridhia, Kapseli) live in different products run by different people. A demo that takes one JSON request and ends with the table (empty groups shown as "<10") or the SPE with the blocked download and its Art. 73(2) log line is something none of them can screen-record in two minutes.
4. Opt-out applied in the open. Art. 71 exclusion is a hard rule in our brief; none of the tools above surface "N opted-out patients excluded from this denominator" at decision time. (Report the aggregate count only, never rows.)
5. A staff view that looks like the EU spec. DAAMS defines decision states (POSITIVE_DECISION_ISSUED, NEGATIVE_DECISION_ISSUED, NEGATIVE_DECISION_APPEALED) and a signed decision document. Using that vocabulary costs nothing and answers "would an access body use this".

## 3. Three candidate unique selling points

### USP A: The explained decision (deterministic rules, paragraph-level citations, 68(3) downgrade)

- Evidence it is a gap: D6.4 declares HDAB internal decision-making out of scope (PDF section 4). DUOS, REMS and HDR UK route or match, they do not cite law. No paper found automates Art. 53/54 assessment. ma3u's citations are block-level.
- Risk it is not: rule-based decisions over 10 known requests can look trivial to a technical juror; Findata staff do this by hand and may say "we do not need software for that"; the hidden jury requests may test 53(2) (private applicant claiming a public-body purpose), which a shallow rule set misses; a look-alike demo (ma3u) exists and could be shown by another team.
- Mitigation: cover 53(2) and 54(a) edge cases; put the quoted paragraph text, from the repo's verbatim articles, in the reason; log the decision in D6.4 vocabulary.

### USP B: Statistics route with a total small-cell rule and a precedent table

- Evidence it is a gap: the open tools default to 3 (DataSHIELD, sdcTable), 5 (OHDSI) or 7 (OpenSAFELY); none shows every requested group with empty ones as "<10"; ACRO checks outputs after the fact, not at query time; percentage and mean suppression from fewer than 10 patients is stricter than NHS HES. Opt-out excluded from every denominator is ours alone.
- Risk it is not: it is the brief's requirement, so every Track 3 team will have some version; the differentiator is rigour (empty groups, percentages, means, halves-up rounding, opt-out denominators), which is hard to see in a video. Ritchie's paper says 10 is convention, not science, so do not claim it is "the standard".
- Mitigation: one slide or README table citing CMS, California, NI HBS and Ritchie; show an empty group as "<10" on screen.

### USP C: The download block you can watch, with its audit log

- Evidence it is a gap: the Austrian SPE is at architecture-principles stage (Endel et al., May 2026); TEHDAS2 D7.4 is non-binding; GÖG has no software before 2028; no open EHDS demo found blocks downloads under Art. 73(2) with a 73(1)(e) access log.
- Risk it is not: this is the weakest USP against vendors. Aridhia, OpenSAFELY and Kapseli have mature airlocks; a two-person prototype cannot rival them and the PwC compliance juror knows that market. The USP is the visible enforcement moment tied to the citation, not the SPE itself.
- Mitigation: demo the attempt, the block, the log line with user ID and timestamp, and the sentence "only non-personal data leaves, Art. 73(2)". Do not claim security properties beyond what the code enforces.

### The AI angle, folded into USP A (not a separate USP)

- Precedent: Al Mandalawi et al. combine an LLM with deny-by-default gates and cited controls; DUOS shows "tool proposes, human decides" holds up against committees (52 of 52, 95 percent).
- Proposed shape: rules choose decision and article list (so the metric cannot be hallucinated); a language model drafts the reason text from the quoted paragraph and the request, and flags borderline cases (53(2), 54(a)) for the staff view. Request text may go to a cloud model; patient rows never do (AGENTS.md hard rule).
- Risk: an LLM-written citation error in front of two lawyers costs more than the AI credit gains. Validate every article number in the drafted reason against the rule engine's list before display.

## 4. Recommendation (PROPOSED)

Lead with USP A, close the demo with USP C, treat USP B as table stakes done rigorously.

- Headline for the video and README: "We build the piece the EU's own DAAMS specification leaves out: the assessment itself, with the article paragraph quoted, and we show what the decision does next."
- Demo order (2 minutes): JSON request in; decision card with quoted paragraph; for R09 the 68(3) downgrade sentence; for a statistics request the table with an empty group as "<10" and the opted-out count excluded; for R05 the SPE, a download attempt, the block, the log line; staff view in DAAMS states.
- Cheap credibility wins: use the HealthData@EU pilot's split (Data Request Form vs Data Access Application Form) as the intake vocabulary; use D6.4 decision states; cite the small-cell precedent table; say what does not work, as the brief demands twice.
- Do not claim: "EHDS-compliant SPE", "the standard threshold", or any comparison number that is not in this file with a URL.
