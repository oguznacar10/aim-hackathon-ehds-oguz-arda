# Landscape depth and go-to-market: is TEHDAS2 our competitor, and how does a two-person team earn money with an HDAB decision engine

Status: PROPOSED. Everything here is a proposal for Arda and Oguz to accept or reject. Nothing is decided.
Written: 2026-10-30, by a research session, extending `docs/research/2026-10-30-competitor-landscape.md` (read first; nothing from it is repeated unless needed for the argument).
Method: three read-only web workers (TEHDAS2 and EU code and timeline; national procurement and vendors; fees, funding and business models) summarised pages; the orchestrating session judged. All pages read 2026-10-30 unless a line says otherwise. "via search summary" = seen only in a search snippet, not on the page. "unverified" = nobody saw it in a primary source. Regulation article wording comes from the StreamLex mirror because EUR-Lex returned empty pages to the fetcher; verify any quote against the Official Journal before it goes in the submission. No worker saw a page that addressed an AI or asked for an action.

## Bottom line

- Arda's worry is half right, aimed at the wrong body. TEHDAS2 does not do what we do: it is a non-binding EU4Health joint action that writes guidelines and specifications, ships no code, and ends 31 December 2026. The people building "almost the same thing" are the national HDAB consortia (Netherlands HDAB-NL builds a DAAMS and an SPE in-house, MVP due August 2026; Sweden pilots a vendor's application-management product; Austria's GÖG prepares all four capabilities until end 2027) and two vendors (BC Platforms, Researchweb). All of them stop at workflow and triage. Nobody found automates the Art. 53, 54, 66(3), 68 assessment with article citations; Sweden's SCB report and the Dutch D5.1 both treat assessment as human committee work.
- A market exists before 2029, but it is public money flowing to public bodies: EU4Health direct grants to member state authorities (EUR 14.4M in 2025; EUR 14.35M "readiness package" and EUR 3M "AI for smart EHDS compliance support" opening 26 November 2026), HDAB designations due 26 March 2027, Commission implementing acts on application and permit templates due 26 March 2027 (draft in consultation until 13 November 2026). A two-person company cannot win that money directly; it gets in as a subcontractor, a consortium partner, or a pilot supplier under direct-award ceilings (EUR 140,000 in Austria, EUR 15,000 federal Germany).
- The only route with a private price anchor today is the applicant side: a failed application costs EUR 4,000 at FDZ Gesundheit (base fee, charged on rejection too) and EUR 1,600 to 2,600 at Findata, France legally forces industry through intermediaries, and no software product for drafting or pre-assessing data access applications was found.
- PROPOSED recommendation: keep the decision engine as the asset and sell it twice. Near term (2026 to 2028): an applicant and data-holder pre-assessment service ("will this pass Art. 66(3) and 53/54, and what would the HDAB cite") priced per application against the fee anchors above. Medium term (2027 to 2029): the same engine as the assessment module inside national DAAMS builds, via integrators and SPE vendors that have no decision layer, with a GÖG HealthData@AT pilot in Vienna as the first reference and the EU4Health "AI compliance support" call as the funding vehicle if an SME can be a partner. Publish the rule engine under EUPL 1.2 (the licence the Commission's own EHDS code uses) so HDABs can adopt it; charge for the hosted pre-check, rule-pack updates as implementing acts land, and integration.

Judgment: TEHDAS2 is a standards body, not a competitor. Its D6.4 DAAMS spec (decision states, signed decision document, structured assessment against Art. 68(1)(a) to (h)) and D6.3 permit templates are the vocabulary every national build will use. We should implement them, which the first landscape file already proposes. The honest answer to "TEHDAS2 does almost the same thing": it specifies the box our engine sits in and explicitly leaves the engine out (D6.4 section 4: HDAB internal decision-making and SPE operation out of scope, per the first landscape file).

### 2c. Honest answers to the two questions

1. Does TEHDAS2 do what we do? No. It specifies (non-binding, no code, ends December 2026) and its DAAMS spec leaves decision logic and SPE operation out. The national consortia and BC Platforms and Researchweb do the workflow around the decision; none automates the decision. Arda's instinct that "someone is doing almost the same thing" is right about the surrounding DAAMS and SPE, wrong about the assessment itself, which nobody found ships and which two public reports (SCB, HDAB-NL D5.1) assume humans do.
2. Does a market exist before 2029? Yes, in three forms. Public: HDAB builds funded by EU4Health direct grants through 2027 and 2028, reachable only as a partner or subcontractor. Private: applicants already pay EUR 1,600 to 4,000 per application at Findata and FDZ today, and France already forces industry through intermediaries. Regulatory: the Art. 70 templates and the Art. 73(5) SPE act are due 26 March 2027, which is when every HDAB has to decide how it will assess; being in the room in 2027 matters more than revenue in 2027.

### 2d. Recommendation (PROPOSED)

One engine, two buyers, in this order:

1. Now to mid 2027: applicant and holder pre-assessment as a hosted service. Input: a draft application. Output: the decision our engine would issue, the article paragraph it would cite, the Art. 68(3) downgrade if applicable, and what to change. Price it under the fee it protects (FDZ EUR 4,000, Findata EUR 1,600 to 2,600). First customers: university research offices and CROs in Austria and Germany, French bureaux d'études, the consultancies already advising on HDH and FDZ (sell to them, not against them). Request text goes to the model, never patient rows (AGENTS.md hard rule holds).
2. 2027 to 2029: the same engine as the assessment module for DAAMS builders and SPE vendors, delivered as EUPL 1.2 open core plus paid integration, rule-pack updates as the implementing acts land, and hosting. First reference: a pilot with GÖG's HealthData@AT in Vienna under the EUR 140,000 Austrian direct-award ceiling. Funding vehicle: EU4Health DI-g-26-24 (text on 26 November 2026), with an authority as lead if SMEs are not eligible.
3. What to say in the hackathon video and README: "We build the assessment the EU's own DAAMS spec and the Commission's open code leave empty, we quote the paragraph, and the same engine tells an applicant in advance what the HDAB will cite." Do not claim a customer, a contract, or any number not in this file.

Why not the alternatives first: licensing to HDABs (A) hits procurement walls and a cultural objection to automated assessment; holder readiness (B) is owned by incumbents and does not use our engine; consulting (D) does not scale. Model C is the only one where a buyer with a budget exists in 2026 and the competitor is a human.

Architecture implication (within the current stack, no locked decision changes): the rules and the verbatim articles in `src/gatekeeper/` must stay a separable library with no web or database dependency, so the same code can run as the applicant pre-check service and inside a third-party DAAMS. Nothing found in this research argues for changing a logged decision.
